# Ideal Completeness, Separable Soundness, and Sequential Extension

## 1. Fourier-block decomposition

After averaging over the random preparation labels, the Fourier labels are uniform classical registers. Define

$$
|\phi_\theta\rangle
=\frac{|0\rangle+e^{i\theta}|1\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$

Conditioned on labels $k,\ell$, the reflection state is averaged over

$$
|\phi_{\alpha+2\pi ks/N}\rangle
\otimes
|\phi_{\alpha+2\pi\ell s/N}\rangle.
$$

The four-phase twirl removes coherences between different excitation-number sectors. The remaining parity-dependent coefficient is

$$
\gamma_b(d)
=\frac2N
\sum_{s\equiv b\ (2)}e^{2\pi i d s/N}.
$$

For $N=2^n$,

$$
\gamma_b(d)=
\begin{cases}
1,&d=0,\\
(-1)^b,&d=N/2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Therefore only the complementary-label block $d=N/2$ contains parity information. Every one-cell marginal is maximally mixed.

## 2. Collision states

In the complementary block,

$$
\sigma_0
=
\frac14|00\rangle\langle00|
+
\frac12|\Psi^+\rangle\langle\Psi^+|
+
\frac14|11\rangle\langle11|,
$$

$$
\sigma_1
=
\frac14|00\rangle\langle00|
+
\frac12|\Psi^-\rangle\langle\Psi^-|
+
\frac14|11\rangle\langle11|.
$$

The direction $|\Psi^+\rangle$ is absent from the support of $\sigma_1$, and $|\Psi^-\rangle$ is absent from the support of $\sigma_0$.

## 3. Honest completeness and zero-error optimality

Complementary labels occur with probability $1/N$. Conditioned on that block, the correct exclusive Bell outcome occurs with probability $1/2$. Hence

$$
p_{\rm correct}^{\rm honest}=\frac1{2N},
\qquad
p_{\rm wrong}^{\rm honest}=0.
$$

The Bell projectors are optimal for zero-error conclusive discrimination. Any correct-even effect must lie in the kernel of $\sigma_1$, and any correct-odd effect must lie in the kernel of $\sigma_0$. The only parity-exclusive directions are the displayed Bell directions.

## 4. Product-effect likelihood ratio

Consider a rank-one product effect generated by local vectors $a$ and $c$. Set

$$
u=a_0c_1,
\qquad
v=a_1c_0,
$$

and

$$
A=|a_0c_0|^2+|a_1c_1|^2.
$$

Its probabilities under the collision states satisfy

$$
4p_0=A+|u+v|^2,
\qquad
4p_1=A+|u-v|^2.
$$

For normalized local vectors,

$$
A+|u|^2+|v|^2=1,
\qquad
|uv|\le\frac14.
$$

Therefore

$$
3p_1-p_0
=
\frac12\left(1-4\operatorname{Re}(uv^*)\right)
\ge0,
$$

and symmetrically

$$
3p_0-p_1\ge0.
$$

Scaling and convexity extend both inequalities to every positive separable effect.

## 5. Complete two-cell measurement

Local dephasing in the Fourier-label registers preserves separability and leaves the parity ensembles invariant. All noncomplementary blocks are identical for the two parities. The product-effect inequalities therefore extend to every separable effect on the complete two-cell Hilbert space.

For a separable POVM with outputs $0,1,\perp$, summing the correct and wrong contributions gives

$$
\boxed{p_{\rm correct}\le3p_{\rm wrong}}.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\Pr[\text{wrong}\mid\text{conclusive}]
=\frac{p_{\rm wrong}}{p_{\rm correct}+p_{\rm wrong}}
\ge\frac14,
$$

or equivalently the conclusive confidence is at most $3/4$.

The factor $3$ is tight. After reading the Fourier labels locally, measuring both reflection qubits in the $X$ basis gives probabilities $3/8$ and $1/8$ for a suitable product effect.

## 6. Why ordinary accuracy has no separation

If every inconclusive result is replaced by a fair guess, the honest joint strategy has success

$$
\frac12+\frac12\cdot\frac1{2N}
=
\frac12+\frac1{4N}.
$$

The tight separable product strategy reaches the same forced-guess value. The resource is witnessed only by the ability to mark a subset of answers as high-confidence while abstaining on the rest.

## 7. Adaptive sequential soundness

### 7.1 Sequential null class

For repeated rounds, a statement only about isolated POVM effects is not sufficient: a general instrument could alter persistent quantum memory. The sequential null class is therefore defined operationally.

The prover begins with memory separable across the two-cell partition and, in every round, applies an adaptive **separable instrument** across the two cell-plus-memory sides. Every branch of such an instrument maps separable states to separable states. LOCC protocols are included. The prover may use arbitrary local ancillas, classical memory, and public-history-dependent choices.

Each new witness pair is independent of the past. The prover must output $0$, $1$, or $\perp$ before receiving the next pair.

### 7.2 Conditional one-round bound

Fix any complete public history $h$ before a fresh round. Conditioned on $h$, the retained memory remains separable, so the effective POVM effects on the fresh pair are separable. Let

$$
c(h)=\Pr[\text{correct conclusive}\mid h],
\qquad
w(h)=\Pr[\text{wrong conclusive}\mid h].
$$

The single-round theorem gives

$$
c(h)\le3w(h),
$$

or

$$
w(h)\ge\frac14\bigl(c(h)+w(h)\bigr).
$$

### 7.3 Successive conclusive answers

Let $\tau_k$ be the round containing the $k$th conclusive answer and define

$$
X_k=\mathbf{1}\{\text{the $k$th conclusive answer is wrong}\}.
$$

For any fixed history before the next conclusive answer, multiply the one-round inequality by the probability that all preceding attempted rounds are inconclusive, and sum over the possible round at which the next conclusive answer appears. This yields

$$
\Pr[X_k=1,\tau_k<\infty\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}]
\ge
\frac14
\Pr[\tau_k<\infty\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}],
$$

where $\mathcal F_{k-1}$ contains the complete history through the $(k-1)$st conclusive answer. Whenever the next conclusive answer occurs,

$$
\boxed{
\Pr[X_k=1\mid\mathcal F_{k-1},\tau_k<\infty]
\ge\frac14.
}
$$

No independence assumption is used.

### 7.4 Lower-tail bound

A binary adaptive sequence whose conditional probability of $1$ is always at least $1/4$ stochastically dominates independent Bernoulli variables with parameter $1/4$. Equivalently, an induction on the number of conclusive answers gives, for every $C$ and $E$,

$$
\boxed{
\Pr\left[
\tau_C<\infty,
\sum_{k=1}^{C}X_k\le E
\right]
\le
\sum_{j=0}^{E}
\binom{C}{j}
\left(\frac14\right)^j
\left(\frac34\right)^{C-j}.
}
$$

If the protocol imposes a maximum of $R$ total rounds, then the event $\tau_C\le R$ is a subset of $\tau_C<\infty$, so the same bound holds with $\tau_C\le R$.

Thus adaptive abstention and variable waiting time do not create a fair-sampling loophole under the stated sequential null class.
